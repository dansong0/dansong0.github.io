As within, so without.  There is a guarantee of suffering in life.  Many people see the alleviation of suffering as the goal.  One of the four noble truths of buddhism is just that: "suffering in this world is endless, but I vow to end it.  I think the most direct way to accomplish this is slightly roundabout.

The more interesting thing is the things that are awesome.  The pains are very acceptable if you make love an inextricable part of your life.  So you see so many products in the marketplace that reduce pain and remove friction.  But where are the products that will bring joy?  Ultimately no product can.  Only you can.  The products that bring you joy have no power intrinsically - they are only vehicles for your own enthusiasm - magnified and reflected back into you.

Cultivating this enthusiasm, this joie de vivre, can only be done through sincere practice.  Sincere practice is prayer.  It is not so important to not suffer.  By immersing yourself in the joy of love and life, by finding your lane, it is not that you are not suffering.  But it is no longer a consideration to you.  You can decenter suffering.

And it can be noted that people who do not, people who choose things, situations, people, that make things more pleasant, often fail to do so.  The reward for hedonism is the treadmill.  It's an important lesson to learn - that way lies futility.  In fact, those same people often become even more sensitive after removing the larger disturbances.  Picture those Victorian royals that would explode if they tasted a dorito.

Sensitivity is not the issue - people who are in their power have a high sense of attunement and awareness.  The problem is that they have failed to decenter suffering.  Ultimately there is a sense that discomfort will obliterate them.  Or, in more gentle terms, they cannot withstand it.  They are not big enough.  But only because they have not had the fortune of experiencing the truly great.  And they have also likely not set the stage for the possibility of an experience.  It's like when a child asks you what love feels like.  There's no words for it, but when you feel it, you realize how small the world is without it.  And yet, that was a world you lived in to some degree of satisfaction.  But now you know better.  I think life is a steady realization of "knowing better".
