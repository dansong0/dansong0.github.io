All my life, I would try and get by on the bare minimum.  One month when I was living in the beautiful city of Sunnyvale, I tried to spend as little money as I could on food that month.  I would buy onions, tomatoes, the occasional green vegetable, canned tuna, and some assortment of garlic and ginger.  My only splurge was fresh salmon here and there.  I spent less than $150 that month.  It was useful, to know if I was really in the trenches, I could feed myself without too much trouble.

At the time of this experiment, I was making 6 figures easily.  In no uncertain terms, I valued the utility of my money more than anything else.  You could call it a scarcity mindset, but it goes deeper than at.  At the heart of any scarcity mindset is a belief that I don't deserve it, and/or that I am vulnerable or in danger.  aside: this is actually an issue I have with the discourse on "scarcity mindset" - where people seem more interested in fixing what is a symptom (the mindset itself) over investigating the cause.

I, for one reason or another, didn't believe that I deserved nice things.  And it was so easy to rationalize.  For one, the excesses of the wealthy were so visible - private jets, private chefs, luxury timepieces - there was something that felt so distasteful about it.  For me, I feel I had projected parts of myself I hadn't had to face yet onto these people, and in one stroke smite both them and my unconscious out of existence.  How could I justify spending two hundred bones on omakase when I have neighbors struggling to make rent?

It's an interesting question.  The short answer is that this is one of the contradictions of life that is not so easily resolved.  To be sure - your 19 course nigiri dinner is not making your neighbor broke.  But a belief that, "I earned this all on my own, and they need to lock tf in", lowkey is manifesting their doom through the aether.  Especially if it's a belief that is held en masse.

So it's good to keep your feet on the ground, even after you get the bag.  Consider those less fortunate.  But if you have the bag, it should enrich you.  And it can't enrich you if you don't believe you are worthy.  It is imperative that you believe that you are worthy of love and a beautiful life.  To do so means you believe that you have and embody the power of a human existence.  It means you believe in more than just the apparent, fiat value of something.  You believe in your judgement in what is true to you.  To be sure, that can change, but you must trust and feel the power of your intuition in that moment.  Often when you choose "wrong", it is just a recalibration, of sorts.
